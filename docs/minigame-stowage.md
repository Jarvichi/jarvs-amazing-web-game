# Mini-game Design — **Stowage** 📦

> Reached **only through the hub world** — a town's crate, not an arcade
> cabinet. Written to `docs/minigame-brief.md`; `docs/minigame-wellspring.md`
> and `docs/minigame-cask-sounding.md` are the two worked examples it follows.

A **packing puzzle** played at a town's crate. A carter's load has been thrown
in loose; you turn the goods over in your head and fit every one of them back
into the crate with nothing left over and no space wasted. No timer, no dice,
no hidden information — the whole problem is in front of you and the only
question is whether you can see the fit.

---

## §1 — Why this game

### The gap in the current roster

Seventeen mini-games ship today. Sorted by what they actually ask of the player:

| Demand | Games |
|---|---|
| Reflex / timing | Crystal Catch, Fishing, Harbour Regatta |
| Pure luck | Lucky Spinner, Marble Run, Marble Race, Fruit Machine, Higher or Lower |
| Luck + shallow decision | Video Poker |
| Real-time strategy | Tower Defence |
| Long-form management | City Builder, Farming Sim |
| Short-term memory | Tile Flip |
| Perfect-information logic | Wellspring |
| Hidden-information deduction | Cask Sounding |

Wellspring and Cask Sounding closed the "thinking game" hole, but they closed
it in the same modality: both are **read the constraints, deduce the answer**
games, played in symbols. Nothing in the roster asks for **spatial reasoning** —
mental rotation, fit, "does that shape go in that hole". That is a genuinely
different faculty, it is the one most people find satisfying without any
tutorial at all, and it is the only major puzzle family the game is missing.

It is also the family that survives interruption best. A packing board holds
its own state visibly: put the phone down mid-solve, come back, and you can see
exactly where you were — which is not true of a deduction board, where half the
progress lives in your head.

### Why a partition-packing puzzle specifically

Four candidates were considered:

| Candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Partition packing** (fit a tray of polyomino goods into a crate) | ✅ **Chosen.** Generated by cutting a solved crate into pieces, so a perfect pack always exists and content never runs out. Two verbs. Scales on shape variance and obstructions independently of size. |
| Tangram / dissection | ❌ Same family but continuous geometry: rotation is no longer four discrete states, which is miserable on touch and impossible to grade. |
| Sliding-block (Klotski) | ❌ Generating a *good* instance means searching for a long optimal solution — the same research problem Sokoban has. Hand-authoring is a finite pile. |
| Nurikabe / Slitherlink | ❌ Another deduction game. The roster now has two; the gap is spatial, not logical. |

Partition packing wins on the property that matters for a live game: **every
board is solvable by construction, because the board is generated from its own
solution** — the same guarantee that makes Wellspring safe.

### Why it belongs in this world

The hub is a realm of towns that trade with each other. Crates sit in the
street of eleven of the thirteen — outside the market hall, on the quay, beside
the works-yard gate — and the game asks you to do the thing anyone looking at
a badly-packed crate wants to do. "Jarv turns up in a town and puts something
right" is already the shape of the hub.

## §2 — Core rules

### The board

A **crate**: a `w × h` grid of slots. Some slots are **dunnage** — packing
timber wedged in the crate that nothing can be stowed on. Everything else must
end up covered.

Beside it, the **tray**: the goods, each an irregular polyomino of 3–6 slots.
Together the goods cover exactly the crate's free slots — no more, no less.

### Input — two verbs

| Verb | Gesture |
|---|---|
| **Take / turn a good** | Tap it in the tray to pick it up. Tap it again (or the ↻ button) to turn it 90° clockwise. |
| **Stow / lift a good** | Tap an empty crate slot to stow the held good there. Tap a stowed good to lift it back to the tray. |

The held good's **anchor** — the first slot of its top row, marked with a dot
in the tray — is what lands on the slot you tap, and a ghost shows the whole
footprint before you commit. Turning is **free**: charging for rotation would
tax exactly the mental work the game is about.

There is no drag. Drag on a phone means the piece is under your thumb while you
are trying to see where it goes, and it needs a second, undiscoverable gesture
for rotation.

### Win condition

> **Every free slot in the crate is covered, exactly once.**

Checked by **reading the crate's occupancy**, never by comparing against the
stored solution. Since the goods' slot-count sums to exactly the free-slot
count, "no overlaps and no empty slots" is the same statement as "all goods
stowed", and any arrangement the player finds counts — including one the
generator never thought of. Overlapping stows are refused at the point of the
tap rather than allowed and scored, so there is no illegal state to check for.

### Scoring

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Par** | The number of goods. Every good has to be stowed once; a perfect run stows each exactly once. Shown from the start. |
| **Stows** | Stow actions actually spent. Lifting is free; re-stowing is what costs. |
| **Manifest** | Optional hint: stows one good where the manifest says it goes, lifting anything in the way. Costs **+3 stows**. |

```
efficiency = clamp(par / stows, 0.4, 1.0)
crystals   = round(tierCrystalBase × efficiency)
           + (stows === par ? CLEAN_STOW_CRYSTALS : 0)
```

Wellspring and Cask Sounding both pay an **under-par** bonus, which they can
because an alternative solution can beat the generated one. Here par is a hard
floor — you cannot stow eight goods in seven actions — so the bonus goes to the
run that *hits* it: a **clean stow**, every good placed right the first time,
no lifts. Same role, achievable shape.

There is no failure state, no clock, and no way to be locked out of finishing.

### What is deliberately absent

- **No countdown.** The roster has four timed games. A clock converts this one
  into a panic game and destroys the "put it down and come back" property that
  is half the reason to build it.
- **No stow limit that ends the run.** Going over par costs crystals and never
  takes the board away. Exploration is how anyone learns a packing puzzle.
- **No score for speed.** Two players who both stow cleanly score the same.
- **No arcade cabinet.** Not in `MiniGameId`, `MiniGamesMenu`,
  `economy.json`'s `miniGameCosts` or `miniGameDailyChallenge.ts`. The crate is
  the only way in.

## §3 — Generation, par, and correctness

### Generating a crate

1. **Place dunnage.** Pick the tier's blocked count at random, rejecting any
   choice that disconnects the free region — a severed pocket of slots is
   usually unpackable and always reads as a bug.
2. **Partition the free region** into `pieces` connected polyominoes by
   multi-source randomised growth: seed every region on a distinct free slot,
   then repeatedly grow a random under-target region into a random unclaimed
   neighbour. Because growth only ever stops when no region has an unclaimed
   neighbour, and the free region is connected, **every slot is claimed** — the
   partition is total by construction.
3. **Repair the tail.** Growth can strand a slot behind a wall of finished
   regions and can leave a region under `minPiece`; both are fixed by merging
   the offender into an adjacent region. A merge can only raise a region above
   `maxPiece`, which is the one thing that triggers a re-roll.
4. **Reject dull boards.** A partition where more than half the goods are plain
   rectangles (a 1×n bar or a 2×2 block) is re-rolled: rectangles fit anywhere
   and turn the puzzle into tidying.
5. **Shuffle the tray** and give every good a random starting rotation. The
   crate itself starts empty.

Solvability needs no solver: the partition *is* a packing.

### Par is exact by definition

`par = goods.length`. There is no cheaper run — each good needs at least one
stow — so unlike Wellspring's par this needs no symmetry reasoning and cannot
be inflated. It does mean par is a floor rather than a target, which is why the
bonus is for matching it (§2).

### Correctness risks worth naming up front

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Growth strands slots**, leaving a partition that does not cover the crate. | The stranded-slot merge in step 3, plus a test asserting over hundreds of seeds per tier that the piece slots sum to the free-slot count, every region is connected, and no slot is claimed twice. |
| **A board generates that cannot actually be re-packed from an empty crate** (it can't — the partition is a packing — but a bug in normalisation or rotation would produce one). | A test *plays* each generated board: stow every good at its generated position and assert the crate reports solved. Then a second test scrubs the stored solution to garbage and asserts the solved-check still agrees. |
| **Rotation normalisation drift** — rotating four times must return the identical shape, or a good slowly walks off its anchor. | Property test: four turns is the identity for every good of every generated board. |
| **Dunnage disconnects the crate**, producing a pocket no good fits. | Connectivity check at generation with re-roll, and a test asserting the free region of every generated board is connected. |
| **Anchor/ghost disagreement** — the ghost showing one footprint and the stow landing on another is the single worst bug available here. | Ghost and stow both call the same `footprint(good, x, y)`; the renderer has no geometry of its own. |

## §4 — Difficulty

Chosen diegetically: **whose load you are packing**. Each is a different crate,
not a modifier on one.

| | **Handcart** — *A carter's load* | **Wagon Bed** — *The market wagon* | **Ship's Hold** — *A trader's hold* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crate | 5 × 5 | 6 × 6 | 6 × 7 |
| Dunnage | 0 | 2 | 5 |
| Goods | 5 | 8 | 9 |
| Slots per good | 4–6 | 3–6 | 3–6 |
| Par | 5 | 8 | 9 |
| Crystal base | 30 | 60 | 110 |
| Barrelled salt | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Solve time | ~1 min | ~2–3 min | ~4–6 min |

Piece-size and placement-count distributions are measured from the shipped
generator over hundreds of boards per tier — see §8, and re-measure if the
grid or piece counts move.

### The knobs, ranked by how much they actually add

1. **Dunnage** *(the real dial)*. An empty rectangle is the friendliest packing
   problem there is: the borders are perfect anchors and the corners tell you
   what has to go where. Wedge timber into the middle and every one of those
   anchors becomes a question. It is the only lever here that changes how hard
   the *thinking* is rather than how long it takes.
2. **Slot-count spread** *(shape variance)*. Goods that are all four slots are
   near-interchangeable; a tray mixing threes with sixes forces you to reason
   about which good is the one that must go in the awkward corner. Cheap, and
   it does real work.
3. **Good count** *(the volume knob)*. More goods is more stows and more
   scanning. It makes a crate *longer*, not harder, and past about ten it makes
   the tray unreadable on a phone.
4. **Crate size** *(also volume)*. Same as above, and it shrinks the slots. 6×7
   is the ceiling at which a slot stays a comfortable tap target in portrait.

### Rejected as difficulty levers

- **A clock.** §2. It converts the genre and kills the put-it-down property.
- **A stow cap that fails the run.** Punishes the exploration that is how the
  puzzle teaches itself. The crystal penalty already does the job.
- **Hiding the tray** (goods revealed one at a time). Turns planning into
  guessing — you can no longer reason about the awkward corner, only hope.
- **Forbidding rotation on some goods.** Reads as a bug, not a rule.
- **Mirroring goods** (allowing a flip as well as a turn). It doubles the
  search space without adding a new kind of thought, and a flip is a third
  verb with no natural gesture.

## §5 — Hub-world integration

### The affordance: a town's crate

Fishing has water, Wellspring has the well, Cask Sounding has the barrel. What
is left that is widely present and unclaimed? Counting `exteriorDecor` across
all thirteen town configs:

| Decor tile | Towns | Total | Claimed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `barrel` | 13 | 80 | Cask Sounding |
| `stoneWell` | 13 | 16 | Wellspring |
| **`crate` / `openCrate`** | **11** | **75** | **— nothing** |
| `sack` | 11 | 25 | a few forage/quest hitRects |
| `smallRock` | 8 | 65 | — |
| `logPile` | 9 | 17 | one quest hitRect |
| `graveStone` | 4 | 25 | one quest hitRect |

Crates are the best remaining fit: 75 of them across eleven towns, none
currently interactive, and a crate is the one piece of street furniture whose

| Town | `crate` | `openCrate` | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|

| Dreadspire Citadel | 0 | 0 → **add 1** | Ship's Hold |
| Gearford | 15 | 8 | Wagon Bed |
| Gravemoor | 0 | 0 → **add 1** | Wagon Bed |

| Saltmere Port | 12 | 4 | Ship's Hold |
| Thornwood Camp | 1 | 0 | Handcart |

The two gaps take an existing `crate` decor tile, so **no new art is needed**.
Tier follows the town's trade: hamlets pack handcarts, the ports pack holds.

### How the tap is wired

No new engine mechanism — an ordinary interactable (`docs/hubworld.md` §7), a
`hitRect` over the crate already drawn there, exactly as the cask hatch
overlays a barrel:

```jsonc
{
  "id": "millhaven-stowage",
  "tx": 34, "ty": 18,
  "reactions": [
    { "type": "dialogue", "text": "Half of this crate is air and the other half is about to fall out." },
    { "type": "screen", "screen": "hub-stowage-hold" }
  ]
}
```

The tier rides in the screen id — `hub-stowage`, `-wagon`, `-hold` — the way
fishing carries its locale variants.

### The stowhand

A crate in the street is easy to walk past a hundred times, and it is the only
door into this game. So every town's crate has a **`<town>-stowhand`** loitering
beside it, with proximity dialogue that changes with state:

| Player state | At 8 tiles | At 4 tiles |
|---|---|---|
| No hook | *"That load's packed by somebody in a hurry."* | *"You'd want a stevedore's hook to shift those — Millhaven's harbour stalls sell them."* |
| Has the hook, crate unpacked | *"That load's packed by somebody in a hurry."* | *"Go on then. See if you can get the lot in."* |
| Already packed today | *"Tight as a drum, that one."* | *"Packed square this morning. Nothing left to shift till the next load."* |

Matched by id suffix (`endsWith('-stowhand')`), so a new town needs no code
change — the same mechanism as `-well-keeper` and `-cellarer`.

### Gating

1. **The Stevedore's Hook** — a one-time tool purchase (90 crystals) from a new
   stall on **Millhaven's harbour row**, beside the ones already selling the
   fishing rod, bait and bucket. Global: bought once in Millhaven, works at
   every town's crate. Checked in `HubWorld.tsx`'s `handleNodeInteract` with
   the same shape as the rod and mallet checks.
2. **Once per town per real day** — `game/hub/stowages.ts`, a direct copy of
   `casks.ts`. Thirteen crates on a daily cooldown is a reason to travel; a
   per-attempt consumable would just be friction on a slow thinking game.

### Rewards

| Reward | Amount | Lands in |
|---|---|---|
| Crystals | tier base × efficiency | The main economy |
| **Barrelled Salt** (new `material` hub-item) | 1 / 2 / 3 by tier | Chef cooking — see below |
| **Town reputation** | +1 | Building upgrades / unlocked services |
| **Clean stow** | +25 crystals | — |

Barrelled Salt is deliberately not a dead-end collectible: it ships with a new
chef recipe, **Harbour Pickle** (`barrelled-salt` + `cask-vinegar` +
`wild-berries`), which also gives Cask Sounding's vinegar its first use.

## §6 — UI design

Portrait-first inside the existing 740px column. The crate is sized
`min(100vw − 32px, 420px)` so a 6×7 slot stays a ~55px tap target.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 📦 STOWAGE               Stows 6 · Par 8    │  MinigameShell
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│           Wagon Bed — the market wagon      │  tier caption
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│      ╔═══════════════════════════╗          │
│      ║ ▓▓▓ ░░  ▒▒  ███           ║          │
│      ║ ▓   ░░  ▒   ███  ▨▨       ║          │  CrateGrid
│      ║ ▓▓  ░   ▒▒  ·   ▨ dunnage ║          │  · empty  ▨ dunnage
│      ║ ·  ·  ·  ·   ·            ║          │
│      ╚═══════════════════════════╝          │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 📦 5/8 stowed · 6 slots open   ↻ TURN       │  StowStatusBar
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  TRAY  [▪▪▪]  [▪▪ ]  [ ▪▪]     selected ↑   │  GoodsTray
│         [▪ ]   [▪▪]   [▪ ]                  │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│   [ 📜 MANIFEST −3 ]      [ ↺ EMPTY IT ]    │  action-btn row
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Slot and good states

| State | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Empty slot | Recessed dark socket, 1px border. |
| Dunnage | Cross-hatched timber fill, no press feedback, `aria-disabled`. |
| Stowed good | Solid crate-goods colour, per-good hue so two neighbours read as two things; rounded only on the shape's outer corners. |
| Ghost (valid) | The held good's footprint at 45% opacity in its own hue. |
| Ghost (invalid) | Same footprint in ember, so a refused tap says *why* before you make it. |
| Tray good, unselected | Mini-grid of its slots on a slat background. |
| Tray good, selected | Gold border + the anchor dot; the ↻ button targets it. |

### Motion

- **Turn:** 120 ms rotation of the tray thumbnail only.
- **Stow:** the good's slots settle in with a 120 ms scale-in.
- **Press feedback:** slots use `filter: brightness(1.35)` — a fixed grid must
  never `translateY` (AGENTS.md); tray goods, which are not in a fixed grid,
  use `translateY(1px)`. Every `:hover` gets an `:active`.
- **Solve:** the crate lid-line sweeps closed, one board pulse, then
  `MinigameResultPanel`.
- All of it behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Accessibility

Every slot is a real `<button>` describing itself and its contents — *"Row 3,
column 4: empty. Activate to stow the held goods."* — and every tray good
announces its shape in slots and its turn state. The status bar is an
`aria-live` region reporting slots left. A DOM board gets all of this free,
which is why the renderer is DOM + CSS grid rather than PixiJS: the same
documented deviation Wellspring made (`docs/minigame-wellspring.md` §7), for
the same reasons — no moving entities, no particle budget, and a canvas would
need a parallel invisible button grid to be usable at all.

## §8 — Measured, not estimated

Measured off the shipped generator, 400 boards per tier:

| Tier | Free slots | Goods | Slots/good (p10–median–p90) | Rectangular goods/board | Legal placements per good (p10–median–p90) | Generation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Handcart | 25 | 5 | 4 – **5** – 6 | 0.8 of 5 | 16 – **36** – 48 | 1.3 ms |
| Wagon Bed | 34 | 8 | 3 – **4** – 5 | 2.2 of 8 | 21 – **50** – 76 | 2.0 ms |
| Ship's Hold | 37 | 9 | 3 – **4** – 5 | 2.3 of 9 | 21 – **46** – 78 | 3.1 ms |

"Legal placements per good" is the count of positions and turns at which a
good fits in an *empty* crate — the size of the search space the player is
reasoning over, and the closest cheap proxy for difficulty this game has.

Two things the measurement changed:

- **§4's claim that dunnage is the real dial survives contact.** The Ship's
  Hold is the biggest crate with the most goods, and its median good still has
  *fewer* legal homes (46) than the Wagon Bed's (50). Five pieces of timber
  take more freedom out of a crate than three extra slots put in — which is the
  whole argument for making dunnage the thing that climbs between tiers.
- **Generation is cheap enough to be synchronous.** At 1–3 ms a board there is
  no need for Cask Sounding's deferred-generation dance (which exists because
  its par solver costs ~0.4 s); the crate is laid out in a lazy `useState`
  initialiser and the screen paints packed and ready.
